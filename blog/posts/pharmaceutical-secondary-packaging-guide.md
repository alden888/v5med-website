## 1. Why Secondary Packaging Deserves Strategic Attention

Primary packaging (the blister, bottle or pouch touching the drug) gets all the regulatory attention. But in practice, **secondary packaging is where most market-entry problems appear**:

- **Regulatory**: Philippine FDA labeling rules require specific information hierarchy, font legibility and English-language content on cartons and inserts.
- **Commercial**: Counterfeit medicines are a documented problem in Southeast Asia. Distributors increasingly demand visible anti-counterfeit features.
- **Operational**: Labels, cartons and trays must work as a *system* — dimensions, adhesives and materials must be engineered together.

Treating these five components as one integrated sourcing project — instead of five separate purchases — eliminates the classic failure modes.

## 2. The Five Components, Specified Properly

### 2.1 Folding Cartons (药盒)

| Specification | Our Recommendation | Why It Matters |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Paperboard | 300–350 gsm SBS / ivory board | Stiffness for automated packing lines |
| Certification | FSC-certified options available | Increasingly required in tenders |
| Printing | CMYK + spot color, matte/gloss lamination | Brand consistency across SKUs |
| Special features | Braille embossing, serialization zones | EU/PH compliance for certain drug classes |

**Common mistake:** Choosing board by price alone. A 250 gsm carton that collapses in Manila's humidity costs more in rejected shipments than the 3% you saved.

### 2.2 Package Inserts / IFU (说明书)

- **Paper**: 40–60 gsm lightweight opaque paper — thin enough for multi-fold, opaque enough for double-sided print.
- **Folding**: Cross-fold vs. parallel-fold must match the carton's internal dimensions *before* printing plates are made.
- **Content**: Multi-language layouts need professional typesetting — machine-translated inserts are a regulatory red flag in the Philippines.

### 2.3 Self-Adhesive Labels (不干胶标签)

| Layer | Options | Tropical Climate Choice |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Facestock | Coated paper / PP / PET | **PP or PET** — paper absorbs moisture |
| Adhesive | Acrylic (general) / hot-melt / freezer-grade | High-tack acrylic rated 10–40°C, 90% RH |
| Finish | Varnish / lamination | Lamination for scuff resistance |

**Pro tip:** Always specify the *application surface* (carton board? PET blister? HDPE bottle?) — adhesive selection depends on it.

### 2.4 Holographic Anti-Counterfeit Labels (镭射防伪标签)

This was the component our Philippine client cared about most — and the one most buyers underspecify. A real security label is a **5-layer engineered system**, not a shiny sticker:

![Anatomy of an anti-counterfeit hologram label: 5-layer structure and pharmacy verification steps](/images/blog/anti-counterfeit-label-anatomy.png)

The four verification layers work together: the holographic foil defeats photocopying, the **tamper-evident VOID layer** defeats label transfer, the **unique serial + QR code** enables per-unit authentication, and microtext defeats casual reproduction.

!> **Counterfeit Alert.** > If your "hologram label" supplier cannot tell you which layer provides tamper evidence, you are buying decoration, not security. Ask for a peel test video before placing the order.

### 2.5 Blister Trays (吸塑托盘)

- **Materials**: PET (clarity, recyclable), PVC (lowest cost, thermoforms well), PP (heat resistance). For pharmaceutical use, specify **medical-grade resin with a declaration of conformity**.
- **Tooling**: Custom thermoform molds are a one-time investment (typically a few hundred to low-thousand USD depending on cavity count). Amortize tooling across annual volume — see our [MOQ vs Unit Price analysis](/blog/posts/moq-vs-price.html).
- **Fit**: Tray cavities must be designed against the *actual device*, with samples validated before mass production.

## 3. Quality Control: What We Check Before Shipment

Every packaging order leaving our network passes a documented inspection. For the Philippine shipment, our QC checklist included:

1. **Carton compression & humidity test** — 24h at 40°C / 90% RH simulation
2. **Label adhesion test** — 180° peel test on the actual application surface
3. **Hologram peel test** — VOID residue verification on every batch
4. **Color matching** — Delta E measurement against approved proofs
5. **Dimensional fit** — insert-to-carton, label-to-panel, device-to-cavity

## 4. Philippine Market Notes

Based on this and previous Philippine projects:

- **Your importer needs an FDA License to Operate (LTO)**, and drug products require product registration with the Philippine FDA before marketing. Packaging artwork should be aligned with the registered product information.
- **Labeling language**: English is standard and accepted; ensure the registered brand name and generic name appear with correct prominence.
- **Climate engineering is non-negotiable**: specify adhesives, laminates and board for hot, humid logistics — Manila warehouses are not air-conditioned by default.
- **Consolidation saves real money**: five packaging components from one coordinated supplier means one QC standard, one shipment, and one customs declaration instead of five.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### 1. What is pharmaceutical secondary packaging?

Secondary packaging is everything that protects, identifies and sells the product without touching the drug itself — folding cartons, package inserts, labels, security seals and blister trays. It carries your regulatory information, branding and anti-counterfeit features.

### 2. Can you customize pharmaceutical packaging with our artwork?

### 3. What is your MOQ for cartons, labels and blister trays?

We offer flexible MOQs for trial orders — see our [MOQ vs unit price analysis](/blog/posts/moq-vs-price.html).

### 4. Can you provide samples before mass production?

Yes. Sample confirmation is standard practice: physical samples or pre-production proofs are shipped for approval before any bulk run. Samples under $500 can be paid instantly via our [secure payment portal](/payment.html).

### 5. Do your hologram labels really prevent counterfeiting?

Our security labels are a 5-layer system: holographic foil (anti-photocopy), tamper-evident VOID layer (anti-transfer), unique serial numbers with QR verification, and microtext. No single feature stops counterfeiting alone — the layered system is what makes replication uneconomical.
