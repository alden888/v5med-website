## Introduction: Why Gauze Quality Matters More Than You Think

Gauze and wound care products are the **most consumed surgical consumables** in any hospital — yet they are frequently treated as commodities. This is a mistake that costs hospitals money and, in worst cases, patient safety.

A single surgical procedure can use **20–60 gauze swabs**, and a mid-size hospital may consume **50,000+ units per month**. When quality varies between batches, the consequences include:

- **Retained surgical swabs** (gossypiboma) — a never-event caused by poor swab integrity
- **Wound infection** — from non-sterile or contaminated dressings
- **Delayed healing** — from lint particles embedded in wound beds
- **Regulatory non-compliance** — missing CE/ISO documentation during audits

This guide covers the **8 gauze and wound care product categories** we manufacture under ISO 13485, with specifications, procurement tips, and quality red flags for B2B buyers.

## 1. Gauze Swabs: The Workhorse of Wound Care

### Specifications
| Parameter | Standard |
|-----------|----------|
| Material | 100% cotton, Type I/II/III |
| Mesh count | 19×15, 20×12, 17×13 per 2.54cm² |
| Sizes | 5×5cm, 7.5×7.5cm, 10×10cm, 10×20cm |
| Ply | 4-ply, 8-ply, 12-ply |
| Sterility | Sterile (EO) or Non-Sterile |
| Packaging | 5s/pack, 100s/pack, or custom |

### Quality Red Flags
- **No X-ray detectable thread** in surgical swabs — mandatory for intraoperative use
- **Lint shedding** — indicates short-staple cotton or poor weaving
- **Whiteness without absorbency** — may indicate excessive bleaching that damages fiber structure

### Procurement Tip
Always specify **mesh count + ply + sterility** in your RFQ. Suppliers quoting "gauze swabs" without these specs are not comparable.

## 5. Non-Woven Sponges

### Specifications
| Parameter | Standard |
|-----------|----------|
| Composition | 70% viscose + 30% polyester |
| Sizes | 5×5cm, 7.5×7.5cm, 10×10cm |
| Ply | 2-ply, 4-ply |
| Sterility | Sterile or Non-Sterile |

### Advantages Over Gauze
- **Lower lint** — 90% less particulate shedding
- **Higher absorbency** — viscose fibers absorb 6–8× more than cotton
- **Softer texture** — less trauma to wound beds during dressing changes
- **Better drape** — conforms to wound contours

### Procurement Tip
Non-woven sponges cost 20–40% more per unit than gauze but reduce wound care time by 30% (fewer dressing changes needed). For chronic wound clinics, the total cost of care is lower.

## 8. Quality Assurance: What to Demand from Your Supplier

### Documentation Package
Every shipment should include:
1. **Certificate of Analysis (CoA)** — batch-specific test results
2. **Sterilization Certificate** — EO cycle parameters (for sterile products)
3. **Material Safety Data Sheet (MSDS)** — cotton composition data
4. **CE Declaration of Conformity** — EU MDR compliance
5. **ISO 13485 Certificate** — quality management system

### Physical Tests to Request
| Test | Standard | Purpose |
|------|----------|---------|
| Absorbency | USP/EP | Measures fluid uptake capacity |
| Lint level | ISO 9073-4 | Particulate shedding assessment |
| Whiteness | CIE Whiteness Index | Bleaching quality check |
| Dimensional accuracy | Visual + caliper | Size conformance |
| Sterility (if claimed) | ISO 11135 | EO sterilization validation |

### Red Flags in Supplier Responses
- "We can provide CoA" without specifying batch → they don't do batch testing
- No mention of X-ray thread for surgical swabs → not surgical-grade
- Refusal to share sterilization parameters → may be using non-validated cycles
- Prices 30%+ below market → likely using recycled cotton or short-staple fiber
