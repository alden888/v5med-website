## 1. Why Peptide Sourcing Is Different from Standard Medical Devices

Unlike syringes or gauze, peptide APIs are **biologically active molecules**. A 99.5% purity claim and a 95% purity claim are not interchangeable — the difference can mean:

- **Safety**: Impurities (trifluoroacetic acid residuals, deletion sequences, oxidation products) can cause adverse reactions
- **Efficacy**: Below-spec potency means failed clinical outcomes or unsellable finished products
- **Regulatory**: Without proper documentation (CoA, HPLC chromatograms, MS confirmation), you cannot legally sell in most regulated markets

> **Key Insight:** Peptides are not commodities. Two suppliers quoting the same molecule at different prices usually differ in synthesis method, purification process, or analytical rigor — not in "margin."

## 2. The 5 Quality Gates Every Peptide Buyer Should Demand

### Gate 1: Synthesis Method Verification
- **Solid-Phase Peptide Synthesis (SPPS)** is the industry standard for research-grade and pharmaceutical-grade peptides
- Ask: Is it Fmoc or Boc chemistry? What resin is used? What coupling reagents?
- Red flag: Suppliers who cannot answer these questions are likely reselling, not manufacturing

### Gate 2: Purity Documentation (HPLC)
- Require **actual HPLC chromatograms**, not just a purity number
- Acceptable: ≥95% for research grade, ≥98% for pharmaceutical grade, ≥99% for injectable applications
- Red flag: "Purity 99%" with no chromatogram = unverified claim

### Gate 3: Identity Confirmation (Mass Spectrometry)
- MS (Mass Spectrometry) confirms the molecular weight matches the expected sequence
- This catches **wrong peptide** errors — the most dangerous type of quality failure
- Red flag: No MS data = no identity confirmation

### Gate 4: Residual Solvent & TFA Analysis
- TFA (Trifluoroacetic Acid) is used in peptide cleavage but is toxic at high levels
- For injectable applications, TFA residuals must be <0.1% (often requiring salt exchange to acetate or chloride form)
- Red flag: No TFA specification for peptides intended for injection

### Gate 5: Cold-Chain Integrity
- Most peptides require **2-8°C storage** (some -20°C for long-term)
- Shipping must use validated cold-chain packaging (gel packs + insulated containers + temperature monitors)
- Red flag: Ambient shipping for temperature-sensitive peptides = degraded product
